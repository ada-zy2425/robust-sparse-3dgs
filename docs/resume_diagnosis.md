# Cross-densification resume diagnosis

The production boundary remains unchanged: formal results come from continuous runs, and shape-changing resume is not qualified. The original 1,400-step test resumed from the same persisted step-700 checkpoint after Gaussian count had changed from 208 to 262; its final continuous/resumed models had 4,418 and 4,442 Gaussians and failed the fixed tolerances.

Protocol v8 compared one uninterrupted step with two independent reloads of the exact same checkpoint. Before the step it checked current `Parameter` identity against every optimizer param group, optimizer state, scheduler state, strategy accumulators, Python/NumPy/Torch/CUDA RNG, camera sample, and rebuilt Scene/Stage references. The continuous and both reloaded branches were exact through the selected camera and contributing forward render. The first numerical difference occurred during backward: maximum tensor differences were `6.92e-8` for continuous versus reload and `4.84e-8` between the two same-condition reloads. After the update the maximum difference was `2.38e-7`. The first structural update still chose the same resulting count in all branches (371 Gaussians).

The trace comparison keeps its raw record but excludes projected `means2d` entries whose radius is zero from the contributing-render comparison: those culled Gaussians do not contribute to RGB or alpha. A targeted CPU regression test ensures that only radius-zero entries are ignored. In the retained v8 execution even the raw forward trace was exact; the rule prevents an earlier v7 run's two culled entries from being mislabeled as an image-producing forward divergence. This is a semantic classification, not a relaxed numerical tolerance.

This evidence excludes a missing checkpoint field, broken optimizer-to-parameter binding, different camera order, different restored RNG, and a stale Scene/Stage reference as the first divergence in this case. It also measures same-condition execution rather than assuming determinism. Protocol v6 then ran all three branches through every subsequent 100-step structural boundary with identical camera sequences. Counts matched at steps 700 and 800. The two same-condition reloads first split at step 900 (877 versus 876); continuous versus the first reload first split at step 1000 (1,407 versus 1,409). By step 1300 the counts were 4,755 / 4,798 / 4,780.

The exercised config has `packed: false` and `sparse_grad: false`. The pinned upstream `RasterizeToPixels3DGSSerialBatchBwd.cu` accumulates RGB, conic, 2D-mean and opacity gradients with `atomicAdd_system`. Together with the exact-forward/same-condition-backward experiment, this is code-level evidence that floating-point accumulation order in the upstream rasterizer backward is the first measured source of perturbation; later thresholded grow/prune decisions amplify it. This project does not rewrite or claim authorship of that CUDA kernel. Changing it would change pinned upstream behavior and require a separate deterministic-kernel implementation and full requalification, which is outside this bounded correction round.

No tolerance was relaxed and no claim of bitwise CUDA determinism is made. The trace tensors and receipts stay in private evidence storage because they contain machine-specific run paths and checkpoint-derived state.
